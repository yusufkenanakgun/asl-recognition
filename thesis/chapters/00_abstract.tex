\begin{abstract}
This thesis presents an end-to-end American Sign Language (ASL)
recognition system that runs on a consumer Android phone and
addresses two tasks: fingerspelling over the 29 classes of the ASL
Alphabet dataset and word-level recognition over the 100-class
WLASL-100 vocabulary. For the letter task, two classifiers are
compared: an EfficientNet-B0 over raw image crops and a compact
multi-layer perceptron over 21 MediaPipe hand landmarks. On the
single-signer ASL Alphabet test split they reach \SI{100}{\percent}
and \SI{99.38}{\percent} Top-1, read as a sanity check rather than a
capability claim; the landmark model is \SI{0.24}{MB}. Re-evaluated
without retraining on an independent \num{870}-image set from
different signers and backgrounds, the image network collapses to
\SI{59.77}{\percent} while the landmark MLP retains
\SI{90.72}{\percent} on detected hands, isolating capture-domain
memorization in the image model and cross-domain robustness in the
landmark model; an on-device evaluation likewise inverts this ranking
on the budget phone, making the landmark variant the deployment
default for letters. For the word task, a bidirectional LSTM is
trained on a two-handed 128-dimensional landmark stream extracted from
a yt-dlp-recovered WLASL-100 corpus (\num{1465} clips, a
\SI{44}{\percent} increase over the \num{1013}-video Kaggle baseline).
A five-seed logit-averaging ensemble reaches \SI{56.22}{\percent}
Top-1 and \SI{81.09}{\percent} Top-5 under the person-mixed split,
clearing the \SI{40}{\percent} acceptance bar by sixteen points; being
person-mixed, this is a benchmark-comparable figure rather than a
signer-independent estimate. A nine-step ablation traces the gain from
a \SI{20}{\percent} baseline to \SI{60.08}{\percent} validation
accuracy across three contributors of comparable weight: data
engineering, the extraction pipeline, and the training recipe. All
four classifiers are exported to TensorFlow Lite and deployed in a
CameraX and MediaPipe Hands Android app; throughput, latency, and the
OOD gap are characterised on a budget (TECNO SPARK 20C) and a flagship
(Xiaomi 15T Pro), yielding a device-conditional model-selection
guideline.
\end{abstract}
